Reasoning & Planning Steps (abridged but complete)

1. 2-D simplification  
   All z–coordinates are identical (-0.05 m), so collision reasoning can safely be done in the XY plane.  For every member we only need:
   • member_id – unique reference  
   • length – to derive its 2-D bounding box  
   • centre.xy – gives its position  
   • first row of the orientation matrix – tells whether the piece runs parallel to X (horizontal) or to Y (vertical)  
   Everything else (width, depth, z, endpoints, full 3×3 matrix) can be ignored for sequencing.

2. Role identification (based on length, height of centre-y, and adjacency)

   • common studs : 2 4 5 7 9 12 14 17  
   • jamb studs   : 16 26 27 28            (vertical 1.95 m, between bottom plate & lintel)  
   • lintels      : 3 13                   (horizontal 1.75 m & 1.75 m @ y = 2 m)  
   • sill trimmer : 11                     (horizontal 1.65 m @ y ≈ 0.7 m)  
   • jack studs below sill : 22 25         (0.65 m high)  
   • jack studs above header (window) : 23 24  (0.45 m high)  
   • jack studs above header (door)   : 19 20  (0.45 m high)  
   • noggings (mid-height blocking)   : 6 8 15 18 21

3. Sequencing strategy  
   • Bottom-Middle-Top: bottom plate first, top plate last.  
   • Middle zone placed strictly Left-to-Right (ascending X-coordinate).  
   • To satisfy the Free-Edge rule each horizontal piece is inserted while its right-hand neighbour stud is still missing; each lintel is inserted while its right-hand jamb is still missing.
     Example: stud 16 → lintel 3 → stud 26.  
   • Noggings are inserted after the left stud and before the right stud in every pair (e.g. stud 9 → nog 21 → stud 12).
