Reasoning Process (abridged but complete)

1. Z–axis simplification  
   Every centre / end-point has z = –0.05 m, so the whole wall is planar.  For collision and sequencing we can ignore the z coordinate and the depth value.  Only the 2-D data (x, y position, length, member orientation and the 50 mm thickness) are needed.

2. Bounding boxes in the XY plane  
   From (centre, length, orientation, width) the two end x–coordinates of each member were reconstructed.  That gives the rectangle that occupies the timber in plan view.

3. Member–role identification  
   • Horizontal at y = 0 m, 6.65 m long  →  bottom plate (id 10)  
   • Horizontal at y = 2.50 m, 6.65 m long → top plate (id 1)  
   • Vertical 2.45 m long (0.025 m clear below & above the plates) → common studs (ids 2,4,5,7,9,12,14,17)  
   • Vertical 1.95 m long (bottom plate → lintel height 2.00 m) → jamb studs (ids 16,26,27,28)  
   • Horizontal 1.75 m long at y = 2.00 m  → lintels (ids 3,13)  
   • Horizontal 1.65 m long at y = 0.70 m  → sill trimmer (id 11)  
   • Vertical 0.65 m long (bottom plate → sill)  → jack studs below sill (ids 22,25)  
   • Vertical 0.45 m long (lintel → 2.50 m top plate) → jack studs above header (ids 19,20,23,24)  
   • Every 0.55 m horizontal at y = 1.225 m → noggings (ids 6,8,15,18,21)

4. Free-edge analysis and sequencing strategy  
   To satisfy the Free-Edge rule a horizontal member must be inserted while at least one end is still open; a vertical member must be inserted while at least one side and one end are open.  
   Therefore each nogging is scheduled after the left stud is in place but before the right stud.  
   Each lintel is scheduled after the left jamb (or king stud) but before the right jamb.  
   Sill is scheduled before the right jamb.  
   The top plate is scheduled last to keep the upper ends of all shortened studs “open”.

5. Left-to-right ordering applied inside the middle zone.
